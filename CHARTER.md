# Community Charter: Cascadia Security Leadership Circle

## 1) Purpose

The Cascadia Security Leadership Circle exists to help Seattle-area security executives solve strategic security challenges through trusted peer exchange, practical collaboration, and selective expert input.

This is a leadership community, not a vendor event series.

## 2) Who It Serves

Primary members:
- Chief Information Security Officers (CISOs)
- Chief Security Officers (CSOs)
- VPs/Heads of Security with enterprise responsibility

Geographic scope:
- Seattle metro and surrounding Puget Sound region for in-person sessions
- Open virtual participation for members traveling or based outside Seattle

## 3) Mission

Advance the effectiveness and resilience of security leadership across the region by creating a high-trust forum for strategic discussion, incident learning, and executive development.

## 4) Core Principles

1. Trust before scale.
2. Peer-first over presentation-first.
3. Practical outcomes over abstract discussion.
4. Curated membership to preserve relevance.
5. Clear conflict-of-interest and vendor participation rules.

## 5) Program Model

- Monthly cadence blending in-person and virtual formats:
  - One in-person leadership roundtable in Seattle area
  - One online topic briefing or workshop
- Quarterly deep-dive sessions on high-priority themes
- Annual in-person summit focused on forward strategy

## 6) Member Commitments

Members agree to:
- Contribute actively to at least 4 sessions per year
- Respect Chatham House Rule for all discussions
- Share practical lessons learned when possible
- Avoid sales behavior in peer forums

## 7) Governance

- Founding Steering Committee (5-7 members)
- Program Lead (operations and agenda design)
- Membership & Ethics Lead (member vetting and policy enforcement)

Leadership terms:
- Steering members serve 12-month renewable terms
- Chair rotates every 12 months

## 8) Membership Policy

Admission criteria:
- Executive accountability for security outcomes
- Willingness to collaborate in a peer environment
- Alignment with ethics and confidentiality standards

Decision process:
- Application + referral or interview
- Steering committee approval

## 9) Confidentiality and Conduct

- Chatham House Rule applies by default
- No recording without explicit pre-session consent
- No attribution of sensitive remarks without direct permission
- Repeated conduct breaches result in removal

## 10) Vendor and Sponsor Policy

- Sponsors may support logistics and neutral research
- Sponsors do not control agenda or membership decisions
- Product demos are not part of core peer sessions
- Any sponsored segment must be clearly labeled

## 11) Success Metrics

Community health:
- Member retention
- Attendance consistency
- Net Promoter Score (NPS) and session usefulness

Impact signals:
- Number of peer collaborations initiated
- Number of reusable frameworks shared
- Evidence of improved security decision quality
